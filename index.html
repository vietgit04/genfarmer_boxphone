What is Boxphone?

The hardware itself: what is inside the box, what people run on it, and why a box beats a shelf of loose phones.

Boxphone (short for Box Phone Farm) is a hardware system that holds and manages dozens to hundreds of smartphone mainboards inside one compact box. Instead of many separate phones, the core components are consolidated into a single box to run automated tasks on social platforms.

Features and structure

  • Optimized design: smartphone mainboards - usually with screens and batteries removed to prevent fire hazards - are stacked or arranged side by side inside a metal enclosure
  • Centralized connection: every device in the box connects to the computer through a single cable and draws stable power
  • Remote control: one piece of software on the computer drives all the phones at once

What people use it for

The system is used for marketing and making money online:

  • Artificial engagement growth: likes, shares, comments or video views on Facebook, TikTok and YouTube to create a crowd effect
  • Account farming: managing a large number of accounts and simulating real user behaviour to avoid bans
  • Advertising and bulk messaging: sending messages or posting content in groups
  • Game and app farming: automating tasks to earn rewards

Why a box beats loose phones

  • Space saving: far more compact than hundreds of complete handsets
  • Higher durability: with the batteries removed - the main fire risk during continuous charging - and the screens gone, the system runs cooler and more stably
  • Centralized management: everything is monitored and maintained from one software interface

What is Change / SuperChange?

GenFarmer's own technology: how one device is made to look like many, and what Super Change adds on top.

What is Change?

Change modifies and emulates Android device fingerprints at ROM and hardware level, so an automation device looks like a real user's phone. In one line: Change turns one device into several distinct devices in terms of system identity. It is pre-installed in the ROM of certain Boxphone models and works only inside the GenFarmer application.

  • ADB hiding: ADB is the channel used to control Android devices remotely. When platforms such as Instagram, TikTok or X detect it, the result is reduced reach, shadowbans or suspension. S8 Change hides ADB so the device looks like an ordinary phone.
  • Upgraded Android ROM: the original Galaxy S8 only supports Android 9, which is old enough to be flagged. S8 Change upgrades the ROM to Android 14, and can go back to Android 12-13 when needed.
  • Diverse fingerprints: identical models, MAC addresses or fingerprints across a farm form a detectable pattern. S8 Change can emulate brands such as Oppo, Xiaomi, Huawei or Vivo instead.
  • Restore and backup: account data such as login state survives a device swap, a reset or a transfer - see Change / Backup / Restore.

What is SuperChange?

Some machines are built to run smoothly for years, others degrade fast, and the difference is the engineering inside rather than the price tag. Super Change is the well-engineered kind: built and optimized at the factory to run smoothly and stay hard to detect.

  1. Change / Backup / Restore - each device gets a new identity plus a backup copy that restores in minutes without interrupting operations.
  2. GPS - location simulation - platforms watch location and movement, so Super Change picks realistic places and simulates natural movement through the day.
  3. Sensor simulation - real phones get picked up and tilted; a perfectly still device stands out, so sensor activity is simulated.
  4. Official app installation sources - apps install as if from the official stores, which reduces errors and feature restrictions, with no need to sign in to Gmail on every device.
  5. ADB hiding - automation still needs a control channel, but the channel stays hidden.
  6. Custom ROM, Android 15, OTA updates - a purpose-built ROM, updated remotely without shipping devices back.
  7. Strong hardware and rare fingerprints - modern, high-spec models rarely seen in phone farms, mostly Snapdragon 835 with 6-8GB RAM and 128GB storage, so the fingerprints are hard to cluster.
Super Change Review: Android 15, 100% Hidden ADB & Real Sensor Emulation

What it changes in practice

  • Fewer checkpoints and fewer bans, most noticeably at scale
  • Scales from dozens to thousands of devices without losing safety
  • Each device behaves like a separate real user
  • Remote updates and fixes instead of downtime

Change is not root. Change modifies how applications recognise the device; root modifies how the operating system works. Change adjusts device parameters at the surface, which is usually enough to pass platform checks, while root goes deep into the system and is easier for Google or YouTube to detect.

Question of the day: What do I need to have ready?

No need to plug anything in yet. Just have every item below ready before you start Day 1.

A place for the box + a dedicated power supply → Wired network + a control computer → Accounts and trial proxies bought in advance → An unboxing video

Preparation

  • A place for the box - a well-ventilated or air-conditioned room, about 10 cm away from the wall so air can flow on all four sides. Do not put it on the floor, inside a closed cabinet, or next to a sunny window.
  • A dedicated power supply - do not share a socket with an air conditioner, oven or washing machine. A UPS of 1,000 VA or more is recommended so the system does not shut down abruptly during a power cut. Have a 3-prong socket ready if you do not have one. Check the voltage your box takes: Box P30 runs on 100-240V, P30+ on 180-240V.
  • A wired network connection - one free port on your modem for the GenRouter. From around 4 boxes up you will also want a LAN switch between the mini PC and the boxes; the documented stable cluster is 1 PC + 1 mini PC + 1 switch + 10 boxes. Details: Router & proxy.
  • LAN cable (if you use a mini PC) - Cat5 or Cat6 (preferred).
  • A control computer - minimum Windows 10 64-bit with a dedicated GPU, at least an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050. Wi-Fi is not required; a LAN connection works just as well. See the table below for how to connect it, and Requirements and safety notes for the full specification.
  • 5 accounts and 5 proxies for a trial run - buy them in advance, do not wait until you need them. You do not need 20 accounts yet.
  • vilao.ai API key - used for automatic commenting. By default the script uses its built-in model; you can switch to another AI model available on vilao if you need to. The fee is about 2% ≈ 50,000 VND, enough for generous use. Sign up and get your key at vilao.ai/console - step-by-step guide.
  • Unboxing video - film yourself opening the package, and keep the seals intact. This is your warranty evidence in case of a manufacturing defect.

The exact specification behind this list - minimum PC configuration, the voltage each box model takes, cable standards and the two rules that void the warranty - is on Requirements and safety notes.

Question of the day: Will my accounts survive?

Start small, start slow. Today you only touch 5 accounts, and most of the time is spent waiting.

Why accounts get banned in clusters

An account is an identity. A proxy is that identity's home address.

If 20 accounts share one address, the platform sees 20 people living in the same house, waking and sleeping at the same time, liking the same kind of content. That is why accounts get banned in clusters rather than one by one.

GenRouter exists so that each phone has its own address. Isolate Mode does one more thing: if a proxy dies, it cuts that phone's network immediately instead of letting the phone expose your real address.

Before you start

  • Plug in both the USB and the LAN cable to the computer or router
How to set up OTG with GenFarmer

Steps

  1. Open the OTG section in the GenFarmer control panel

  2. Add the two address ranges

    Add 192.168.4.1-255 and 192.168.5.1-255 so the software knows where to look for the devices.

  3. Select the devices and click Activate

Fix: Boxphone indicator light error

A red or flickering light is a hardware fault on one mainboard. How to tell the slot from the board.

Symptoms: the indicator light flickers or shows red, and the device drops out of the software.

The normal light code is blue for USB mode and green for OTG mode. Red or flickering is a hardware fault on that mainboard, not a software problem.

How to fix it

  1. Swap the mainboard to another slot

    Remove the mainboards that are misbehaving and swap their positions with others. This tells you whether the fault follows the slot or the mainboard.

  2. Reseat the FPC cable

    If the problem persists after the swap, adjust or reseat the FPC cable - the long orange ribbon running along the mainboard.

Reseating the FPC cable means opening the box. If you are not comfortable doing it, contact support first: hardware altered without guidance falls outside the warranty, as noted on Requirements and safety notes.

4. Preparing proxies

An account is an identity. A proxy is that identity's home address. If 20 accounts share one address, the platform sees 20 people living in the same house, waking and sleeping at the same time, liking the same kind of content - which is why accounts get banned in clusters rather than one by one.

What follows from that:

  • One proxy per phone. A full box of 20 phones needs 20 proxies
  • For a trial run, 5 accounts over 2-3 proxies is acceptable, but that is not a production setup
  • Isolate Mode is the safety net: when a proxy dies, expires or errors, that phone is cut off from the internet immediately instead of falling back to your real address
  • Besides assigning the proxy, GenRouter also hides WebRTC, DNS, MAC address, location and timezone, blocks traceroute and stops LAN scanning

Spotify package

The Spotify package: increasing stream counts, and the checklist to clear before the first run.

What is in the package

Spotify is a single package rather than a Trust and Boost pair: it farms accounts with a real listening history and turns that into plays for the tracks you point it at.

  • Plays around the clock. The system searches out songs and artists at random the way a listener would, varies how long each track is listened to, and builds playlists itself when there are none yet - so the listening history looks like a person's rather than a script's.
  • Songs chosen to fit the pattern. Tracks are picked to match what that account has been listening to, not dropped in at random.
  • Seeding songs and playlists. Liking, listening through to the end, skipping - the ordinary mix of actions, performed automatically.
  • Scale without the pattern. Hundreds of accounts running at once, each on its own device and address, which is what keeps the behaviour from clustering.

What counts as a valid stream

A play only counts if all of these are true, which is why a browser cannot do this job and a phone farm can:

  • Listened to for more than 30 seconds
  • Natural behaviour, no repeated identical actions
  • The device is not rooted and ADB is hidden - see What is Change / SuperChange
